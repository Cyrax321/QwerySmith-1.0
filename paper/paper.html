QwerySmith: Parameter-Efficient Domain Specialization and the Few-Shot Paradox in Small Language Models for Text-to-SQL

Cyrax • Autonomous Intelligence Research • cyrax8590@gmail.com
Abstract

Translating natural language questions into executable Structured Query Language (Text-to-SQL) is an essential prerequisite for democratizing relational database access. While contemporary frontier models (≥70B parameters) exhibit high syntactic competence, their enterprise deployment is severely constrained by latency, memory overhead, and strict on-premises data privacy governance. Small Language Models (SLMs, ≤ 4B parameters) present an attractive alternative for edge deployment; however, their performance under standard few-shot in-context learning is plagued by syntactic hallucinations and degradation.

In this work, we introduce QwerySmith 1.1, a specialized 4-billion parameter Text-to-SQL model built on Qwen3-4B via Unsloth-accelerated Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation (QLoRA). Across rigorous multi-split benchmarks, QwerySmith 1.1 achieves an in-distribution execution accuracy of 88.5% (95% CI: [78.2%, 94.3%]), outperforming the 3-shot foundation baseline by +21.3% ($p < 0.001$), while Exact Match string parity leaps from 7.0% to 84.5% (a 12-fold relative gain). On the held-out enterprise gretel_test benchmark, QwerySmith reaches 55.7% execution accuracy, recording 43 head-to-head pairwise wins versus 18 losses (+25 net wins, $p = 0.0019$).

Crucially, our multi-matrix diagnostic evaluation uncovers and formalizes the Few-Shot Paradox in Constrained Decoders: in compact autoregressive models, prepending few-shot exemplars dilutes limited attention capacity over schema tokens, causing an empirical accuracy drop of up to 9.1% compared to zero-shot inference. By internalizing grammatical constraints directly into low-rank adapter weights, QwerySmith completely sidesteps prompt dilution, lowers inference latency by 3.2×, and reduces schema-to-clause structural distortion (Frobenius error drops from 2.45 to 0.78). We release the open-weights adapter, 16-bit merged model, and 4-bit GGUF edge binaries.

1. Introduction

Relational databases store petabytes of transactional, operational, and financial intelligence. Interfacing with these repositories historically required specialized proficiency in SQL. Text-to-SQL systems aim to bridge this divide by automatically translating unstructured human queries into executable database statements.

While commercial closed-source frontier models demonstrate remarkable semantic parsing capabilities, enterprise adoption faces three strict roadblocks: (1) Data Privacy (sending confidential database schemas to third-party endpoints is prohibited in regulated sectors); (2) High Latency (few-shot prompting inflates input contexts to 1,000–3,000 tokens); and (3) Financial Cost. Small Language Models (≤4B) offer private, instantaneous local deployment on commodity hardware.

2. The Few-Shot Paradox in Compact Decoders

Theorem 1 (Attention Dispersion in Constrained Receptive Fields).
Let $\mathcal{S}$ denote database schema tokens, $\mathcal{Q}$ the user prompt, and $\mathcal{E}_k$ be $k$ demonstration exemplars. For autoregressive decoders with bounded dimension ($d \le 2560$), adding $k$ in-context exemplars increases attention entropy over non-schema tokens, diluting the cumulative attention mass assigned to active relational constraints: $$\sum_{j \in \mathcal{S}} \alpha_{t,j}(\mathcal{E}_k) < \sum_{j \in \mathcal{S}} \alpha_{t,j}(\emptyset), \quad \forall t \in \mathcal{Y}_{\text{gen}}$$

Empirically, on gretel_test, Base 0-shot achieves 52.3% execution accuracy, but 3-shot prompting degrades performance to 47.3%. On heldout_sqale, performance collapses from 50.0% down to 40.9%. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning embeds the grammar directly into adapter weights, enabling robust zero-shot inference without token penalties.
